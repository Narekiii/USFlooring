# Website Redesign Prompt — US Flooring & Molding Inc.
### Warm. Bold. Built as a Funnel to Drive Calls and Estimates.

Create a complete modern website redesign for **US Flooring & Molding Inc.**, a family-owned flooring store and flooring contractor at:

**1508 W Burbank Blvd, Burbank, CA 91506**
Phone: **(818) 515-0009** — `tel:+18185150009`
Fax: (818) 840-8222
Business type: Flooring store / flooring contractor
Reputation: **5.0 rating, 15 Google reviews**
Products/services: Hardwood, laminate, vinyl, moldings, flooring materials, professional installation, free estimates, showroom consultation

This is not a brochure site. It is a **conversion funnel**. Every section has one job in addition to informing the visitor: move them one step closer to calling, requesting an estimate, or walking into the showroom. Nothing on the page should just "sit there" — it should build trust and then ask for the action.

The whole site is structured around Simon Sinek's **Golden Circle: WHY → HOW → WHAT**, in that order, because people don't buy flooring — they buy confidence in a decision. Lead with the emotional reason, earn trust with the craftsmanship story, and only then show the product catalog.

---

## Tone Shift: Warmer + Bolder

The original brief was polished but a little safe. Push it further in two directions at once:

**Warmer** — this should read like it was written by someone who's stood in a customer's living room, not a marketing agency. Use first-person plural ("we," "our family"), specific human details (Vick's father, age seventeen), and language about *feeling* — what a floor does to how a home feels, not just how it performs.

**Bolder** — bigger type, higher contrast, shorter punchier headlines, fewer hedging words. Replace soft suggestions ("Talk to a Flooring Expert") with confident, direct commands ("Get Your Free Estimate — No Pressure, No Guesswork"). The site should feel like a firm handshake, not a polite brochure.

Both directions serve the same goal: warmth builds trust, boldness converts that trust into a call.

## The Golden Circle Funnel

Think of the homepage as three funnel stages stacked in order. Each stage ends in a soft or hard CTA — never a dead end.

### STAGE 1 — WHY (Emotional Hook → Build Trust)
**The reason US Flooring exists.** Choosing flooring is stressful — cost, durability, timelines, who to trust. This section says: *we exist to take that stress off your shoulders.*

> **Your floor isn't just a surface. It's the first thing your family feels underfoot every single day. We help you get it right.**

This is where the visitor decides "these people get it." Funnel action: keep scrolling, or hit the primary CTA if they're already sold.

### STAGE 2 — HOW (Craftsmanship → Build Credibility)
**How they deliver on that promise.** Generations of hands-on craftsmanship, Vick learning the trade at seventeen from his father, honest pricing, a 1-year labor guarantee, realistic timelines, no upselling.

This is where skepticism turns into confidence. Funnel action: mid-page CTA — "Talk to Vick's Team" or "Get a Free Estimate."

### STAGE 3 — WHAT (Products → Convert)
**What they actually offer.** Hardwood, laminate, vinyl, moldings, installation, showroom consultation — now shown with confidence because trust is already built.

This is where browsing turns into action. Funnel action: hard CTA — "Request Your Free Estimate," "Call Now," "Book a Showroom Visit."

Every visitor who scrolls the full homepage should hit **three** distinct calls to action, each stronger than the last.

## Design Direction — Warmer, Bolder Palette

Keep the warmth of the original palette but raise the contrast and confidence:

- Warm Ivory (background): `#F5F1EA`
- Deep Charcoal (primary text): `#1E1E1C`
- Walnut Brown (headings/accents): `#6B4A2D`
- **Bold Terracotta (new — primary CTA color):** `#B5502E`
- Sand Beige (secondary surfaces): `#D8C4A8`
- Warm Gold Accent (highlights): `#C89B5A`

The terracotta CTA color is new and deliberate — it should be the *only* color used for primary action buttons, so it becomes visually synonymous with "take action" across the whole site.

Typography: large, confident headlines (bold serif or slab-serif for headers pairs well with the warm/craftsmanship feel), clean sans-serif for body copy, generous white space so the boldness doesn't tip into clutter.

Avoid: cold corporate blue, generic contractor red/black, over-luxury styling, cluttered grids, dated template feel.

---

## Copywriting Rules

**Do:** short bold headlines, first-person plural, specific human details (Vick, seventeen, the father), direct commands as CTAs, honest confident claims backed by the guarantee and reviews.

**Don't:** "cheapest flooring in town," "we are the best," "guaranteed lowest price," generic "transform your space" filler, hedgy or apologetic phrasing anywhere near a CTA.
